Item 1 -- Post-Sapling fd-cache/bufsize re-test: Added a true nofdcache baseline and fixed a timeout bug in bench_matrix.sh, ran the full 12-trial A/B at heights 600K-900K. Result: no measurable throughput win from either -perffdcache or -perfbufsize, cleanly isolated this time. Closes the question -- disk I/O's remaining CPU share is genuine read/write time, not open/close overhead, at both pre- and post-Sapling heights.

Item 2 -- Memory profiling: Used vmmap/heap/malloc_history (CLI-native, sidestepping Instruments' GUI-only export). Found AddToBlockIndex's permanent block-index map dominates allocation (~66% of tracked ThreadImport allocation), Groth16 verification allocates essentially zero heap despite dominating CPU, and -- the most interesting catch -- that vmmap's headline Physical footprint figure is confounded by macOS memory compression over a multi-hour run, producing a spurious "growth rate is slowing down" pattern that didn't survive cross-checking against Writable regions: Total. Net: memory grows roughly linearly with chain length, no leak signature.

Item 3 -- Sprout/Sapling anchor root-existence index: Implemented HaveSproutAnchorAt/HaveSaplingAnchorAt through the full CCoinsView chain, matching Zebra's membership-check technique. Full regression clean (284/284 Boost tests; 205/207 gtest, with both remaining failures confirmed as pre-existing test-order flakiness via baseline comparison).

Item 4 -- Groth16 batch-verification scoping: Confirmed no batching in the pinned bellman, confirmed modern zkcrypto/bellman has real batch-verification code, confirmed the underlying miller_loop primitive needed to hand-port it already exists in the pinned crate, confirmed the idle-thread angle is a queue-wiring gap not a proof-system limitation.

Everything is written into Perf.md (new §6 and §7) and committed to perf-401. Remaining open items per §0 are now just NEON blake2b integration, the -O1/-O2 comparison, and the (much larger) Groth16 batch-verification implementation itself.
